# Scientific limitations

CardioSim Lab is an educational, reduced-order model. It is **not** clinically validated and must not be used for diagnosis or patient-specific prediction. This file lists what is mechanistic, what is reduced-order, and where the output is known to deviate from human data.

## What is governed by state equations and what is schematic

| Layer | Implementation | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Ventricular single cell (Ionic cell tab) | ten Tusscher–Panfilov 2006 (TP06), 19 state variables, Rush–Larsen, dt 0.02 ms | **mechanistic (ionic ODEs)** |
| Tissue action potentials (all views on the main clock) | Reduced-order waveform; APD set per cell from a restitution map (Mitchell–Schaeffer fit) | reduced-order |
| Activation | Event-driven graph propagation on a 2.5 mm voxel grid; anisotropy, refractoriness, CV restitution | reduced-order (mechanistic sequence, no source–sink) |
| AV node, slow pathway, accessory pathways | Lumped directional links with a recovery-dependent delay (Sun et al. 1995 recovery term) | reduced-order |
| SA / junctional / ventricular pacemakers, ectopic foci | Imposed cycle lengths with overdrive reset (foci: protected, not reset) | schematic automaticity |
| ECG | Lead-field forward model (infinite homogeneous medium) for chest leads; Einthoven lead vectors on the heart dipole for limb leads; scalar torso gains | reduced-order volume conductor |
| Great vessels in the 3-D view | Geometry for orientation only | illustration (no electrical role) |

Local APD90 of a cell is not the surface QT: the QT spans earliest ventricular activation to latest ventricular recovery across the heart.

## Known deviations from human data

- **PA interval** (~47 ms) is longer than the reference ~28 ± 7 ms.
- **Precordial morphology:** V2–V3 show deep S waves and V4 can be fragmented (near-field effect of an infinite-medium lead field close to the idealised heart).
- **RBBB:** QRS ≈ 145 ms with terminal anterior/rightward forces, but the frontal area-based axis is distorted (≈ −140°). Status: *incomplete*.
- **LBBB:** QRS ≈ 140 ms (meets the width part of the Strauss et al. 2011 criterion, doi:10.1016/j.amjcard.2010.11.010) after introducing a nominal retrograde Purkinje–muscle junction delay (40 ms). Mid-QRS notching is not assessed.
- **Retrograde Purkinje entry:** myocardium → Purkinje conduction uses a separate nominal delay (40 ms) so that ectopic beats and LBBB spread mainly cell-to-cell (broad QRS). It was calibrated to QRS widths, not measured.
- **Left anterior fascicular block** does not produce left-axis deviation in this geometry.
- **RV mass** ≈ 26 % of ventricular cells (relatively high).
- **APD restitution (revised in Phase 2b):** APD = APD_ref · S(CL_mem) · (1 − 0.35·e^(−DI/50 ms)). S reproduces the human steady-state slope (23 ms per 100 ms CL, Franz et al. 1988, doi:10.1172/JCI113706); the fast term and the per-cell memory (weight 0.25 per beat, i.e. adaptation over ≈ 4–8 beats instead of the minutes reported in humans) are nominal shape choices. The previous single map produced unphysiologically short APDs (≈ 135 ms) for premature beats. S below CL 400 ms is an extrapolation.
- **Autonomic coefficients, nodal and pathway parameters** are nominal (marked "nominal" in the parameter panel); only directions of effect are meaningful.
- **Ectopic and focal rhythms:** automaticity is imposed by a rate/coupling parameter, not generated by ionic currents.
- **Re-entry** works through lumped links (AVNRT, AVRT). The pathways are joined at the His bundle; there is no lower common pathway.
- **Atrial flutter:** the circuit uses an idealised spherical RA with non-conducting tricuspid-annulus, caval and intercaval regions and a slow CTI (CV × 0.2, APD × 1.3; nominal). Cycle length ≈ 180 ms (≈ 330/min). Flutter-wave morphology on the surface ECG is not validated.
- **Atrial fibrillation:** driven by a pulmonary-vein focus at 450/min (CL 133 ms; the median shortest PV CL in CAPLA was 126 ms, doi:10.1016/j.jacep.2023.08.018) in atria with seeded APD heterogeneity (SD 40 %) and CV 0.45 m/s. The atrial reference APD (300 ms) is calibrated so the emergent RA cycle length (≈ 170–240 ms) is near reported AF cycle lengths (CS AFCL 171 ± 27 ms, Verma et al. 2008, doi:10.1016/j.hrthm.2007.09.027). Wavelet dynamics in an event-driven graph (no source–sink or curvature effects) are qualitative. The irregular ventricular response relies on a nominal concealed-conduction rule in the AV node.
- **Scar-related VT:** a prototype (transmural scar, slow isthmus, slow border zone) did not give clinically plausible behaviour — spontaneous re-entry around the scar at CL ≈ 190 ms, or no inducibility — so it remains unsupported (parameters hidden).
- **Unsupported (not runnable):** scar-related VT, polymorphic VT, torsades de pointes, VF, hyper-/hypocalcaemia, EAD/DAD propagation, drug-specific arrhythmias. See `docs/scenario-registry.json`.

## Ionic model specifics (TP06)

- Equations transcribed from the gotran/CellML translation in cbcbeat (`tentusscher_panfilov_2006_epi_cell.py`, `_M_cell.py`); epicardial and M cells differ only in g_Ks (0.392 vs 0.098).
- **Endocardial variant entered from the published description, not from a machine-readable file:** g_to = 0.073; s_inf = 1/(1+exp((V+28)/5)); τ_s = 1000·exp(−(V+67)²/1000) + 8. The endocardial and M-cell initial conditions are approximate. Pacing for 20–30 beats reaches only approximate steady state (slow [Na⁺]i drift).
- **Calcium — the cell model is deliberately not used for the calcium scenarios.** In our runs TP06 lengthens APD when [Ca²⁺]o rises and shortens it when [Ca²⁺]o falls — the opposite of the clinical QT–calcium relationship. Measured here at CL 1000 ms, 30 beats (`scripts/dev/cao.ts`):

  | [Ca²⁺]o (mM) | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | APD90 epi (ms) | 255 | 283 | 306 | 324 | 337 |
  | APD90 M (ms) | 301 | 351 | 393 | 426 | 451 |
  | APD90 endo (ms) | 245 | 274 | 299 | 317 | 331 |

  Bartolucci et al. (Front Physiol 2020;11:314) show this is a general defect of models whose L-type calcium-dependent inactivation sits in the saturated part of its sensitivity curve, including O'Hara–Rudy and Grandi. The hyper- and hypocalcaemia scenarios therefore take the **size and sign of the tissue effect from human measurements** (30.1 ms of QT per mmol/L of total serum calcium; Young et al. 2021, Mendelian randomisation in UK Biobank, n = 76 226), not from the ionic model. The calcium slider remains in the Ionic cell tab so the disagreement is visible rather than hidden, and a regression test pins both facts. The slope is linear and was measured in populations near the reference range, so the scenarios at 1.6 and 3.2 mmol/L are extrapolations and probably **understate** the true change: the equivalent slope against *ionised* calcium in haemodialysis patients is roughly threefold steeper (8.55 ms per 0.1 mmol/L; Kim et al. 2019).
- **EADs and DADs — reproduced, but only after modifying the cell model.** Blocking IKr alone produces no EADs in TP06 at any degree of block (epi APD90 231 → 286 ms from gKr 0.5 → 0, no EAD). EADs appear only with the published modification that shortens the ICaL f-gate time constant and so enlarges the window current (τ_f × 0.5 with ICaL × 2.5 and IKs × 0.2: APD90 815 ms with a secondary upstroke at 393 ms; Vandersickel et al. 2014). DADs likewise do not arise from calcium loading alone; a background RyR leak must be added (leak 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ ms⁻¹ with ICaL × 2, SERCA × 4.5, NCX × 2.5 gives a triggered beat at 403 ms at CL 600 ms). **These are modifications of the model, not emergent properties of it**, and the scenario and UI text say so. The afterdepolarisations exist only in the single cell: the tissue receives their measured *timing* as an ectopic stimulus, because the ionic model is not coupled cell-to-cell.
- **Drug block is static.** Drug scenarios use the measured IC50 values of the CiPA ion-channel panel (Crumb et al. 2016) with the Hill equation at an assumed Hill coefficient of 1 (the source reports IC50 but not Hill coefficients for these channels). Use-dependence, state-dependent binding, trapping, metabolites and protein-binding variation are not modelled, and TP06 has no late sodium current — so late-INa blockers (ranolazine, mexiletine) are deliberately absent rather than modelled misleadingly. The torsade-risk category shown for each drug is clinical knowledge attached to the scenario text, **not a model output**: this model cannot predict whether a drug causes torsades.
- **Tissue mapping:** ionic results reach the tissue only as an APD scale (mean APD90 of epi/M/endo cells) and a CV scale (√ of the dV/dt_max ratio, from cable theory). The tissue AP *shape* is fixed, so:
  - a peaked T wave from faster repolarisation (hyperkalaemia) is not transferred;
  - U waves are not representable;
  - use-dependent Na⁺-channel block is not modelled;
  - Ca²⁺-channel-blocker effects on the AV node are not modelled.

## Measurement rules (arrhythmia-aware)

- **PR** is reported only when the model confirms that the P wave and the QRS belong to the same antegradely conducted impulse. It is suppressed for:
  - ventricular-origin complexes;
  - escape complexes;
  - re-entrant/echo complexes.
  The reason is shown per beat.
- For **QRS ≥ 120 ms** a note recommends JT (QT − QRS); JT is reported.
- **Rhythm-level analysis:** atrial activity is read from the model's activation records at three atrial sites (high RA, low RA, posterior LA) over 6 s. When the atrial cycle length is < 250 ms (an app display rule, not a diagnostic criterion):
  - regular activity (CV < 6 %) is reported as a flutter-wave rate with the AV conduction ratio;
  - irregular activity is reported as fibrillatory, and the table shows the mean ventricular rate and RR variability instead of a single RR; PR is not shown.
- **Detector:** QRS offset at 2.5 % of the peak spatial slope (captures slow terminal forces); onset extended to the PR-segment minimum of the spatial magnitude for delta waves.
- **Detector limits:**
  - For broad ectopic complexes and fast wide-complex tachycardia, the detected QRS end can merge with the T wave. Model truth (first to last ventricular activation) is shown alongside.
  - A dissociated P wave falling on a QRS can widen the detected QRS.
- **Delta-wave onset** is detected from the rise of the spatial ECG magnitude above its PR-segment minimum.
- Values are reported to the resolution of the sampling interval (2 ms at 500 Hz). Model "truth" values are internal activation/recovery times, not ECG readings.

## Ventricular re-entry: what sets the cycle length

This engine spreads activation as discrete events on a graph with refractoriness. It has no
source–sink mismatch, no wavefront curvature and no electrotonic coupling, and the consequence is
specific and measurable: **functional re-entry has no excitable gap**, so a rotor's period is just
the refractory period,

    CL(re-entry) ≈ erpRatio × apdFloor × APDref

Measured across 18 fibrillation runs at reference APDs of 300–380 ms (`scripts/dev/vf-scan2.ts`),
the ratio CL/APDref was **0.408–0.422** against a predicted 0.414. Three things follow.

- **Fibrillation cycle length is a calibration, not a prediction.** With the default floor the
  model fibrillates at ≈ 150 ms, far faster than human VF. The VF scenario raises the floor to 0.79
  so the local activation interval is 220 ms, against 218 ± 24 ms measured in vivo in humans
  (Nair et al. 2012) and a dominant frequency of 4.5–6.4 Hz (Bradley et al. 2011). The cost is that
  physiological APD shortening at short diastolic intervals is suppressed.
- **Anatomical re-entry has to be protected from functional re-entry.** The scar scenarios raise
  the floor to 0.60 so that faster functional rotors cannot outrun the circuit around the scar.
- **A premature beat cannot break a wavefront here.** Single-extrastimulus initiation failed in all
  30 R-on-T combinations tried (coupling 280–400 ms × dispersion 0.15–0.25 × two sites). Every
  re-entrant scenario therefore needs burst pacing, whereas clinically a single R-on-T beat often
  suffices.

### Torsades de pointes: which mechanism, and what is reproduced

Torsades was first attempted as **re-entry**, and that route failed:

1. **Initiation** — a late-coupled R-on-T beat on a long-QT, dispersed substrate never produced
   re-entry in any of 30 combinations (coupling 280–400 ms × dispersion 0.15–0.25 × two sites). A
   single premature beat cannot break a wavefront in this engine.
2. **Morphology** — when re-entry was instead started with a burst, the axis rotated at chance
   consistency, i.e. randomly polymorphic.
3. **Rate** — those rhythms ran at 166–200 ms, fibrillation rates rather than torsades rates.

Dessertenne's **two competing foci** was tried next and also failed on the waveform: it produced a
single fade rather than repeating spindles (lead II peak-to-peak 1.82 → 0.24 mV, then flat), with
narrow spiky complexes, because the two foci only capture part of the ventricle at that rate.

The scenario now uses a **drifting source** — the origin migrates one step per beat around a
seven-site path, a reduced-order stand-in for the drifting rotor that current optical-mapping and
simulation work holds responsible for the undulating axis (a Doppler effect; reviewed in Front
Cardiovasc Med 2024;11:1363848). Every beat captures, the tracing is continuous with no isoelectric
baseline, and the lead II amplitude waxes and wanes about ten times per run with a 5-fold swing.

It is still marked **incomplete**, for two reasons. The complexes are sharper and more fragmented
than the broad smooth deflections of a real recording. And the rate, ≈ 176 /min, sits at the slow
edge of the clinical 200–250 /min — because organised ventricular capture fails above ≈ 176 /min on
a long-QT substrate here: driving faster blocks every other beat (measured 50 % capture across
cycle lengths of 130–300 ms). Obtaining full capture also required lowering the ERP/APD ratio from
0.92 to 0.85 — both nominal values, but a tuning choice made to get the appearance.

What the scenario does **not** do is explain why a long QT predisposes to torsades. The migrating
source is imposed, not generated by the prolonged repolarisation, so it cannot be used to predict
torsade risk.

Two measurement notes. The analyser treats activations within 80 ms as one fused QRS; without that,
two foci firing 8 ms apart were counted as two beats, which doubled the apparent rate and turned a
smooth rotation into a zigzag. And axis-rotation metrics alone were the wrong target: a single
126° sweep scores well on rotation consistency while looking nothing like torsades. What the eye
reads is the **amplitude envelope** of one lead, so that is now measured directly.

### Rotation does not separate torsades from polymorphic VT here

Net rotation divided by total rotation ("straightness") cleanly separates organised polymorphic
rhythms from fibrillation — 0.89 for torsades and 0.94 for the polymorphic VT scenario, against
0.18 for fibrillation. It does **not** separate torsades from polymorphic VT: both rotate
coherently. In this model the two differ by setting (long QT with triggered foci versus a scar),
rate, and whether the rhythm self-terminates — not by the twist itself.

### Polymorphic VT versus fibrillation

The model does not draw a sharp line between them. The two scenarios differ in degree of
organisation and rate — axis dispersion ≈ 61° at ≈ 310 ms for polymorphic VT against ≈ 85° at
≈ 220 ms for fibrillation — rather than by distinct mechanisms.

### Baseline ECG in the scar scenarios

The slow border zone distorts the tracing before any arrhythmia is induced: in sinus rhythm the
monomorphic scar substrate gives QRS ≈ 112 ms with QT ≈ 560 ms, and the polymorphic one QRS ≈ 132 ms
with QT ≈ 506 ms. Some widening is expected after a large infarct, but these are at the extreme end
and are an artefact of applying one slowing factor to a 6 mm rim of tissue.
